Layout rules

How every back-office screen chooses between a full page, a drawer, a dialog and a detail page. The full conventions are in ux-review/COMPONENTS.md.
A. Full pagePrimary workLists, reports and work screens use the whole width. Reference tables scroll sideways on a phone; work lists reflow into cards so the amount, status and action stay in view.Used on: Journals, Payments, Inventory, Approvals, every Settings list.
B. Side drawerA record with contextThree to five fields, or a record with its history, and the list stays in place behind a scrim. It never sits beside the table, so the table is never squeezed.Used on: Add location, Add user, Record a stock count, Approvals review.
C. DialogA bounded questionOne or two fields, a reason, or a confirmation with a consequence. Centred, as tall as its content, closes with Escape and returns focus to the button that opened it.Used on: Rename a unit, Change a price, Reopen a period, Link a transfer to a sale, Switch off a cash point account.
D. Detail pageComplex recordsA record with several parts or a real workflow opens on its own page with a breadcrumb. The list keeps its filters.Used on: New dispatch, Receive stock, Airline invoice, a journal.
E. Small screensStack, never squeezeThe side menu becomes a strip along the top; section tabs scroll with a hint. Drawers fill the width. List screens become labelled cards. Reference tables scroll on their own.Checked at: 390, 768, 1024, 1440 and 1920 px.

Which one to use

QuestionIf yes
Does the person need to compare rows?Full page
Is it one or two fields, a reason, or a confirmation?Dialog
Is it a short form of three to five fields, or a record with context?Drawer
Would a panel beside the table make the table hard to read?Never a split: drawer over a scrim, or a full page
Is it a multi-part form or a step-by-step job?Its own page with a breadcrumb

The Cash Point sale screen keeps its two-part layout on purpose: the item list and the running bill must be visible together while a cashier works.

Actions, forms and feedback

One primary action per screen, in the header or the status banner. Row actions are secondary: one bordered action at most, the rest quiet, destructive ones never loud. Forms show every error at once, under the field, with a count at the top and focus on the first. Choices of up to six options are chips, dates are date pickers, and optional fields say so. Confirm only what is hard to undo. A reversible switch acts at once and says what happened. Success appears as a dismissible banner; an empty list explains itself and offers a way out. Navigation lives in one place (sync.py): a few groups, then a quiet second row. No screen reaches a sibling through a header button.
